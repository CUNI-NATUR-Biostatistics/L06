## Inspiration consulted

- Relevant sources from `_internal/obecne/nove/biostatistics_course_inspiration_hub.md`: Modern Statistics with R, the CUNY Biostatistics book, genomicsclass/PH525x, and ModernDive; the Stage 0 visual pattern from Experimentology was retained.
- Dataset-selection pattern worth borrowing: ModernDive and Experimentology both preserve the regression language when the predictor becomes categorical: raw observations first, fitted group means second, residuals attached to those fitted values, and reference-group coefficients interpreted as mean differences. CUNY then contrasts the overall mean with group means before asking the omnibus ANOVA question.
- What to avoid from those sources: A test-selection catalogue, formula-first dummy-variable algebra, high-dimensional genomic machinery, several predictors or interactions, simulation as the main data story, and a dataset whose dependence, transformation, count response, or missing-data burden would displace the categorical-predictor lesson.

## Numerical and practical feasibility probes

### Palmer penguins

- Provenance, reuse conditions, and stable access: The `palmerpenguins` package provides both a simplified table and the original fields imported from the Environmental Data Initiative. The data are CC0, the primary ecological study is identified, and the package supplies stable CSV access. Version 0.1.1 is already recorded in this repository's `renv.lock`.
- Observational unit, intended population, and sampling story: One row is one adult foraging penguin measurement. The 344 rows have 344 unique `studyName` plus `Individual ID` keys and span three species, three islands, and 2007-2009. The intended lesson population must be stated narrowly as the sampled Palmer Archipelago penguin community rather than all individuals of each species everywhere.
- Numerical probe: Of 344 rows, 342 are complete for body mass and species: Adélie `n = 151`, mean `3700.7 g`, SD `458.6`; Chinstrap `n = 68`, mean `3733.1 g`, SD `384.3`; Gentoo `n = 123`, mean `5076.0 g`, SD `504.1`. In the Adélie-versus-Gentoo entry, the Gentoo coefficient is `1375.4 g` with ordinary 95% CI `1260.7-1490.0 g`; the equal-variance two-sample t-test gives the identical estimate, interval, `t = 23.61`, and `df = 272`. With all species, `F(2, 339) = 343.63`, `p < 2.2e-16`. Tukey simultaneous intervals show Chinstrap minus Adélie `32.4 g` (`-126.5, 191.4`, adjusted `p = 0.881`), Gentoo minus Adélie `1375.4 g` (`1243.2, 1507.5`), and Gentoo minus Chinstrap `1342.9 g` (`1178.5, 1507.4`).
- Visual and assumption probe: Raw jittered observations plus fitted group means are legible for both the two-species and three-species versions. Residual Shapiro-Wilk `p = 0.051`, Bartlett `p = 0.050`, maximum Cook's distance `0.027`, and largest-to-smallest group variance ratio `1.72`; no single point or severe variance mismatch takes over the lesson. Species and island are partly inseparable: all Chinstrap observations are from Dream and all Gentoo observations from Biscoe, while Adélie occurs on all three islands. Sex counts are nearly balanced within species, and adding sex changes the Gentoo-versus-Adélie coefficient only from about `1386 g` to `1378 g` in sex-complete rows; the sex coefficient is about `668 g`. Island and sex therefore become explicit limitations and a natural L07 question, not silent claims of species causation.
- Teaching opportunities and complications: The selected ordering should first compare Adélie with Gentoo so the two-group coefficient and t-test identity are visually clear, then add Chinstrap to reveal that a strong omnibus result need not imply that every pair differs. Do not begin with Adélie versus Chinstrap, because the near-zero difference would make the initial two-group geometry less motivating. Use course-native Czech labels and recreated graphics. Any palmerpenguins artwork used later must retain the requested “Artwork by @allison_horst” credit even though the data themselves are CC0.

### Chick weights by feed

- Provenance, reuse conditions, and stable access: `datasets::chickwts` is distributed with R and documented against Snedecor 1948 and McNeil 1977. Stable technical access is excellent, but the underlying numerical data lack a separate modern reuse statement, so source citation would be retained.
- Observational unit, intended population, and sampling story: One newly hatched chick was randomly allocated to one of six feed-supplement groups and weighed after six weeks. The randomized assignment supports treatment-effect language more directly than the observational penguin data.
- Numerical probe: All 71 rows are complete; group sizes are 10-14. Mean weights range from `160.2 g` for horsebean to `328.9 g` for sunflower. The overall model gives `F(5, 65) = 15.37`, `p = 5.94e-10`. With casein as reference, estimated differences range from `-163.4 g` for horsebean (95% CI `-210.3, -116.5`) to `5.3 g` for sunflower (`-39.4, 50.1`). Tukey results contain both supported and unsupported pairs, but there are 15 rows to interpret.
- Visual and assumption probe: Six raw-data strips and fitted means remain readable in a full-width plot but are crowded for a first categorical model. Residual Shapiro-Wilk `p = 0.627`, Bartlett `p = 0.660`, maximum Cook's distance `0.094`, and variance ratio `2.82` are acceptable.
- Teaching opportunities and complications: The design is analytically clean and causal, but choosing only three of six feeds would look selective while presenting all six would make reference coding and post-hoc output unnecessarily dense. The historical animal experiment and unfamiliar supplements also need more context than the penguin question.

### PlantGrowth

- Provenance, reuse conditions, and stable access: `datasets::PlantGrowth` is distributed with R and cites Dobson 1983, but neither the organism nor the two treatment identities are documented. The underlying numerical data have no separate modern reuse statement.
- Observational unit, intended population, and sampling story: The documentation describes 30 plant cases under a control and two treatment conditions, with 10 observations per condition. A broader target population and the treatment mechanisms cannot be stated from the available metadata.
- Numerical probe: Mean dried weights are `5.032` for control, `4.661` for treatment 1, and `5.526` for treatment 2. The overall model gives `F(2, 27) = 4.85`, `p = 0.0159`. Reference-level ordinary 95% CIs include zero for treatment 1 minus control (`-0.371`, `-0.943 to 0.201`) and treatment 2 minus control (`0.494`, `-0.078 to 1.066`), while the Tukey interval for treatment 2 minus treatment 1 excludes zero (`0.865`, `0.174 to 1.556`, adjusted `p = 0.012`).
- Visual and assumption probe: Three raw-data strips with fitted means are clear. Residual Shapiro-Wilk `p = 0.438`, Bartlett `p = 0.237`, maximum Cook's distance `0.198`, and variance ratio `3.22` do not force a later-course remedy, although two observations exceed the rough `4/n` Cook's-distance screen.
- Teaching opportunities and complications: The contrast between the omnibus result and the one supported Tukey comparison is pedagogically excellent. It is still weaker than Palmer because students cannot interpret what either treatment means biologically, and the tiny anonymous data story would become a statistical exercise rather than a scientific question.

## Selected dataset

- Name/source: `palmerpenguins::penguins`, collected by Kristen Gorman and Palmer Station LTER and documented in Gorman, Williams, and Fraser (2014), with data imported from the Environmental Data Initiative under CC0.
- Response variable: `body_mass_g`, presented to students as `hmotnost_tela` in grams after explicit removal of the two rows missing body mass.
- Key predictor: `species`, presented as the factor `druh` with the reference level chosen deliberately rather than left as an unexplained R default.
- Why this one is best for this week: It combines the strongest biological relevance and documentation with the cleanest teaching sequence. Adélie versus Gentoo makes the two-group coefficient and t-test identity concrete; adding Chinstrap creates an informative overall-versus-pairwise distinction without changing the formula. Unlike the two experimental alternatives, every group name and response unit has immediate scientific meaning.

## Minimal data story

- Biological question in one sentence: How does mean body mass differ among the three penguin species sampled in the Palmer Archipelago, and which species differences are supported after asking the overall three-group question?
- First figure idea: Show individual Adélie and Gentoo body masses, add one horizontal fitted mean per species, and reveal each residual as the vertical distance to its species mean; then add Chinstrap using the same axes, colours, fitted-mean marks, and residual language before showing the overall and pairwise questions.
- Bridge to next lesson: Ask whether the apparent species differences would change after accounting for sex, and use the island-species sampling structure to motivate why L07 adds predictors and distinguishes an unadjusted group difference from a coefficient estimated while controlling another variable.

## Why this dataset matches the project filter

- Supports model-first teaching: The entire lesson can use `lm(hmotnost_tela ~ druh)`, with the t-test, coefficient table, ANOVA table, and Tukey follow-ups presented as questions about one fitted model rather than isolated procedures.
- Understandable for biology beginners: Species identity, body mass, individual penguins, group means, and differences in grams are concrete and require almost no domain jargon.
- Uses real data appropriately: The observations come from a published modern ecological study with complete metadata and CC0 data. The lesson will distinguish association from causation and state the sampled community, island structure, years, missing values, and sex limitation.
- Avoids unnecessary tooling or syntax burden: The teaching table requires only two columns after a transparent base-R complete-case step; no new package syntax, transformation, join, repeated-measures structure, or synthetic scaffold is needed for the main analysis.
